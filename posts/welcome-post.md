## Interpreting the brief

During this stage, I began looking understanding what the client, BlaBla Corp is actually asking for, even the things that are not explicitely stated. The brief shows that information and experience are equally important, showing that useful content is not the only thing needed for this new tool we have to create.

## First idea: cybersecurity awareness

One of my first possible directions was a cybersecurity awareness community.I was interested in the psychology behind everyday digital risks, especially how non-expert users respond to scams, phishing emails, suspicious SMS messages or QR codes in public places.

After some research and checking some forums, it turns out phishing through the phone is the most common threat people come across in their workplaces. Instead of thinking about cybersecurity in general, I was considering non-expert users who have corporate jobs who are exposed to  scams, either by their work email, SMS, QRs on public places. Since the information they hold in their power is important, and probably companies don't want it to be compromised.

After seeing this issue, I started thinking about possible solutions to address this issue. Instead of following fixed methods, I explored different approaches inspired by design toolkits, allowing me to think broadly and consider multiple directions.

Some early ideas are related to creating an interactive platform that helps users recognize online dangers and develop safer digital habits. Maybe create a community with this purpose. 

## Initial functional requirements

From this idea, I identified several possible functional requirements:

*Possible requirement -> Why it could matter*
- Users could share examples of scams or suspicious messages -> This would make the platform community-based rather than only informational.
- Users could browse educational resources -> Non-expert users would need simple explanations before making decisions.
- Users could comment or react to posts -> Discussion could help users compare experiences and learn from others.
- Users could report suspicious content -> This could make the platform feel practical and connected to real situations.

## Risks and Trade-Offs
Also, I would have to consider what is most valuable here. Increasing interactivity may affect performance, but making the security concepts too simple could reduce accuracy and usefulness. Additionally, allowing user-generated content introduces risks related to misinformation, so that would have to be considered as well.
Another important point would be to be sepecially careful because users might share inaccurate advice or misinterpret scams. If the platform allowed users to upload screenshots of emails or messages, there could also be privacy issues, since those screenshots might contain names, email addresses, phone numbers or workplace information.

## Reflection before choosing a final direction
At this point, cybersecurity awareness is only an initial direction, not a final concept. Next week i will speak with my teammate and put in common the general topic as well as the ideas we came up with. Hopefully we will follow a security-awareness focus for this prototype. Until next week.
